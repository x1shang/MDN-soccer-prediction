From two early positions of a penalty kick, we estimate where the ball crosses the goal plane and quantify uncertainty. We integrate gravity, quadratic drag and Magnus lift, then train a noise-conditioned residual mixture density network on airborne trajectories drawn from a mixture of uniform launches and priors informed by tracked flights, including Asai's curve kicks.

For the stated kick at an assumed $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ coordinate noise, the model gives a mode of $(Y,Z)=(2.67,1.79)\,\mathrm{m}$ and joint $50\%$ and $95\%$ region areas of $0.106$ and $0.619\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Conditional on airborne crossing, the estimated probability of crossing within a goal rectangle inset by one ball radius is $97.7\%$.

Of $1{,}225{,}000$ simulated launches, $1{,}091{,}052$ cross in flight. On $109{,}108$ held-out crossings the median error is $0.266\,\mathrm{m}$ at $5\,\mathrm{mm}$, and joint coverage is $50.1\%$ and $95.1\%$. Aiming fills the high-speed sampling grid more densely. Point errors change little on shared uniform-prior flights and on three Asai kicks evaluated with the two models trained without Asai anchors. In a separate paired experiment, including rebound and rolling crossings in training reduces median error on post-contact crossings from $0.242$ to $0.163\,\mathrm{m}$ at $5\,\mathrm{mm}$.

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Problem Restatement}

Take the penalty spot as the origin and the goal as the rectangle $|Y|<3.66\,\mathrm{m}$, $0<Z<2.44\,\mathrm{m}$ in the plane $x=11\,\mathrm{m}$ \cite{ifab}. From $\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,0.11)\,\mathrm{m}$ and two later observations, the contest asks for the most likely crossing point and its uncertainty. The stated case gives \cite{upc2026}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf r(0.05)=(1.50,0.45,0.44)\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
  \mathbf r(0.10)=(2.96,0.86,0.74)\,\mathrm{m}.
  \label{eq:given}
\end{equation}
Unknown spin and aerodynamic forces make extrapolation from two points ambiguous. With a fixed start, $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ independent coordinate errors give a second-difference acceleration uncertainty of $4.47\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. For the stated kick, the same noise gives a median radial deviation of about $0.34\,\mathrm{m}$ under quadratic extrapolation (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}).

\subsection{Background and Approach}

Wind-tunnel tests of non-spinning balls found drag coefficients near $0.43$ and $0.15$ on either side of the drag crisis, at Reynolds numbers around $2.2$--$3.0\times10^5$ \cite{asai2007}. A later ball comparison reached $3.3\times10^5$ \cite{asai2013}. Free-kick fits give $C_D=0.25$--$0.30$. In the same study ten free kicks had speeds of $23.0$--$28.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ \cite{bray2003}. Trajectory studies find lift coefficients of order $0.2$--$0.3$ under some conditions and estimate aerodynamic curves from tracked flights \cite{goff2010,goff2017}. These results inform our choices of prior bounds and lift law.

We compare a quadratic extrapolation, a Bayesian reference and an MDN \cite{bishop1994} under shared flight dynamics. We fit simulation priors using tracked Asai, ISSIA-3D and DFL trajectories and digitised Barber--Carr\'e data \cite{asai2020,soccernetv3d,issia,dfl2025,barber2010}. Held-out simulations assess coverage. We assess transfer to the three Asai kicks using reduced-mixture models trained without their anchors.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Assumptions and Approximations}

Forecasts use the known start, fixed observation times and specified coordinate noise $\sigma$. The main endpoint distribution conditions on reaching the goal plane before ground contact, denoted by $E$. Section~\ref{sec:bounce} also considers crossings after ground contact. We report the density mode for the stated case and use the mean for point-error scoring. We score geometric in-frame arrival when the ball centre crosses within $|Y|\le 3.55\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.11\le Z\le 2.33\,\mathrm{m}$, allowing one ball radius at each boundary.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{A1.}] \textbf{Ball.} A rigid sphere has mass $0.430\,\mathrm{kg}$, radius $0.110\,\mathrm{m}$ and cross-section $A=\pi R^2$ \cite{ifab}. Buoyancy is omitted (Section~\ref{sec:forces}).
  \item[\textbf{A2.}] \textbf{Air.} The main model uses still air at $\rho=1.20\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.
  \item[\textbf{A3.}] \textbf{Drag.} Each flight has a constant $C_D$ in $[0.15,0.30]$, a range that includes reported free-kick and supercritical wind-tunnel estimates \cite{bray2003,asai2007}.
  \item[\textbf{A4.}] \textbf{Lift.} Transverse spin determines lift. The slope and cap in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lift} are chosen to match the measured order of magnitude \cite{goff2010,asai2007}.
  \item[\textbf{A5.}] \textbf{Spin.} We hold spin fixed, following the short-flight approximation discussed by Team~358 \cite{team358}. Section~\ref{sec:shift} tests a $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ decay time.
  \item[\textbf{A6.}] \textbf{Measurement.} The start is exact. The two observations have independent Gaussian coordinate errors with common standard deviation $\sigma\in\{2,5,10,15\}\,\mathrm{mm}$ in evaluation.
  \item[\textbf{A7.}] \textbf{Prior.} Table~\ref{tab:prior} gives the common simulation bounds. The Bayesian reference uses independent uniforms and the main training set uses the mixture in Section~\ref{sec:splits}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Model}
\label{sec:forces}

Let $\mathbf u=\mathbf v-\mathbf w$ be the velocity relative to air, $\mathbf g=-g\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, and $V=4\pi R^3/3$. The force balance combines weight, quadratic drag, transverse lift and Archimedean buoyancy \cite{nasaforces,nasadrag,nasalift,archimedes}.
\begin{equation}
  m\dot{\mathbf v}=m\mathbf g-\tfrac12\rho A C_D|\mathbf u|\mathbf u
  +\tfrac12\rho A C_L|\mathbf u|^2\hat{\mathbf n}-\rho V\mathbf g.
  \label{eq:full}
\end{equation}
Buoyancy is $0.066\,\mathrm{N}$ upward, or $1.6\%$ of the ball's weight. Omitting it gives
\begin{align}
  \dot{\mathbf r}&=\mathbf v,\label{eq:kin}\\
  \dot{\mathbf v}&=\mathbf g-\frac{\rho A C_D}{2m}|\mathbf u|\mathbf u
    +\frac{\rho A C_L}{2m}|\mathbf u|^2\hat{\mathbf n}.\label{eq:dyn}
\end{align}
We use a three-dimensional spin vector $\boldsymbol\omega$ and define
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{n}}=
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{u}}{|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{u}|}, & |\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{u}|>0,\\[2mm]
    \mathbf{0}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  C_L=\min\!\left(1.4\,\frac{R\,|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{u}|^2},\,0.30\right).
  \label{eq:lift}
\end{equation}
Spin about $+\hat{\mathbf z}$ gives the special case $\hat{\mathbf n}\propto\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u$. Lift vanishes when the cross product is zero. Bray and Kerwin give a related vector model for free kicks \cite{bray2003}.

\subsection{Numerical Solution and Event Detection}

The synthetic flight simulator uses fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4), a $2\,\mathrm{ms}$ step and a $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ horizon. Linear interpolation locates $x=11\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z=R$, retaining goal-plane crossings before contact. Reintegrating $1{,}024$ sampled launches per million-scale dataset at $1\,\mathrm{ms}$ leaves the crossing status unchanged and changes the recorded endpoints by at most about $0.012\,\mathrm{mm}$. The ISSIA model-form check uses \texttt{solve\_ivp}. Tracked-flight prefix forecasts use RK4 with a maximum $5\,\mathrm{ms}$ step.

\subsection{Bayesian Inference of Launch Conditions}

Let $\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\mathbf{v}_0,\boldsymbol{\omega},C_D)$ and let $\mathbf{y}_1,\mathbf{y}_2$ be the two observed positions. With the start fixed and independent Gaussian errors,
\begin{equation}
  p(\mathbf{y}_1,\mathbf{y}_2\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})
  =\prod_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{3/2}}
   \exp\!\left[-\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_i-\mathbf{r}(t_i;\boldsymbol{\theta})\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right].
  \label{eq:lik}
\end{equation}
Four-stage adaptive importance sampling combines a $20\%$ prior component with a Gaussian proposal. The first three stages use possibly widened likelihoods to adapt the proposal by weighted moments. The fourth stage alone supplies the posterior at the specified $\sigma$, with weights proportional to likelihood times $p(\boldsymbol\theta)/q(\boldsymbol\theta)$. We use $40{,}000$ draws per stage for the stated kick and report the conditional effective sample size (ESS). A weighted Gaussian kernel estimate on a $160\times160$ grid gives the mode and joint highest-density regions (HDRs) \cite{hyndman1996}.


\subsection{Million-Scale Simulation Dataset}
\label{sec:splits}

We generate $1{,}225{,}000$ launches per sampling strategy. Before flight integration, the full mixture assigns $40\%$ to independent uniform draws, $24\%$ to two DFL penalty anchors, $12\%$ to three Asai curve-kick anchors and $24\%$ to aerodynamic anchors from eleven other DFL segments, three ISSIA arcs and one digitised Barber--Carr\'e trajectory. Each anchor stores fitted launch and aerodynamic parameters. For the five penalty and curve-kick anchors we perturb all seven fitted parameters, using a wider Gaussian for $30\%$ of the draws. For the other fifteen anchors we draw launch speeds separately, transport the fitted spin into the sampled horizontal heading, and perturb the spin and drag. We clip to Table~\ref{tab:prior} and randomly mirror launches laterally, including the corresponding axial-spin components.

In the aimed version (v3), we select $65\%$ of the non-Asai anchor draws with $v_x\ge24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. For a nominal flight time $11/v_x$, we choose uniform ballistic targets in $Y\in[-3.85,3.85]\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Z\in[0.55,2.65]\,\mathrm{m}$ to set the transverse launch velocities. We rotate spin with the change in horizontal heading and integrate the full flight equations to obtain labels. The v2 comparison uses the same base mixture without ballistic aiming. For the two Asai-source holdouts, we replace all Asai-derived draws with the same independent uniform draws. The contest observations are used only for the final query and a descriptive count of nearby simulated prefixes.

\subsection{Wiener Residual Mixture Density Network}
\label{sec:mdn}

Let $\mathbf b_{\mathrm{lin}}$ be a gravity-only crossing estimate based on midpoint velocity and let $\mathbf b_{\mathrm{quad}}$ be a regularised quadratic estimate from the two observed positions and the known start. The feature extractor limits extrapolation times and uses the linear time estimate when the quadratic time is invalid. We blend the endpoints using a Wiener shrinkage weight,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf b_\sigma=\mathbf b_{\mathrm{lin}}+w_\sigma
     (\mathbf b_{\mathrm{quad}}-\mathbf b_{\mathrm{lin}}),\qquad
  w_\sigma=\frac{s_*^2}{s_*^2+5\sigma^2/\Delta t^4},\qquad
  s_*=7.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}} .
\end{equation}
The denominator incorporates the second-difference acceleration variance (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}). The fixed scale $s_*$ sets the strength of shrinkage. The network uses 22 features, including velocities, acceleration, apparent drag and lift, extrapolation times and endpoints, $w_\sigma$ and $\sigma$. The features are standardised using training data.

A $192$-unit input projection and two $192$-unit residual blocks use LayerNorm and GELU \cite{layernorm2016,gelu2016}. The head produces five correlated bivariate Gaussian components,
\begin{equation}
  p(\mathbf q\mid\mathbf y_1,\mathbf y_2,\sigma,E)
    = \sum_{k=1}^{5} \pi_k
       \mathcal N\bigl(\mathbf q;\mathbf b_\sigma+\boldsymbol\delta_k,
                        \boldsymbol\Sigma_k\bigr),\qquad
       \mathbf q=(Y,Z).
  \label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
The six outputs per component give a softmax weight logit, a two-dimensional residual $\boldsymbol\delta_k$, two positive scales $s_{kj}=\operatorname{softplus}(r_{kj})+10^{-3}$ and correlation $\rho_k=0.98\tanh(r_{k\rho})$. The diagonal of $\boldsymbol\Sigma_k$ is $(s_{k1}^2,s_{k2}^2)$ and its off-diagonal entry is $\rho_k s_{k1}s_{k2}$.

Each dataset uses three runs (seeds 11, 42 and 155), AdamW with initial learning rate $0.002$ and cosine decay, batch size 512 and 100 epochs \cite{loshchilov2019}. Each epoch draws a new $\sigma\sim U[1,18]\,\mathrm{mm}$ per trajectory and adds independent Gaussian noise to the six coordinates. We choose the checkpoint by selection-set negative log-likelihood (NLL). On the independent calibration fold, separately at each test noise level, we fit a common scale multiplier by NLL and adjust the nominal $50\%$ and $95\%$ highest-density region (HDR) thresholds using ranks from 256 predictive draws per flight \cite{hyndman1996,hpdsplit2022}. We score physical-coordinate NLL, Euclidean errors of the predictive mean, and empirical coverage on the held-out test split. The data manifests record source and simulator hashes, split counts and dataset hashes. Computations use NumPy, SciPy, PyTorch and Matplotlib \cite{harris2020,virtanen2020,paszke2019,hunter2007}.

\subsection{Comparison Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

We repeat the model comparisons on fixed subsets of the v3 splits, drawing $65{,}536$ training, $4{,}096$ selection and $8{,}192$ test trajectories without replacement (seed 20261108). Each candidate uses the same 60-epoch budget, three training seeds and optimizer, with the specified interventions below. Checkpoint selection minimises mean selection NLL at 2, 5, 10 and $15\,\mathrm{mm}$. The full-mixture checkpoints supply the main forecast. They are scored on the same test subset as a reference trained with a larger budget.

Arms A and B use six coordinates plus $\sigma$. C and D use sixteen physical features, and E adds the quadratic endpoint to A's inputs. A and C predict absolute coordinates. B, D and E predict residuals about the quadratic endpoint, skip examples with no finite quadratic prediction during training, and use a zero endpoint in that case during evaluation. Arms A--E use the same three-layer, width-192 GELU body and five components. We also retrain the legacy coordinate MDN (two 64-unit SiLU layers, three components) and PI-ResMDN (three 128-unit SiLU layers, five components and its original 16 features and quadratic fallback) on these v3 rows.

For architecture screening, we compare 11 and 16 physical inputs for the direct target, and vary mixture size and depth using the 22-feature Wiener residual target. Depth is screened with SiLU. A shallower candidate is preferred when its NLL disadvantage is within one paired seed standard deviation and the mean absolute HDR coverage gap rises by at most 1.5 percentage points. We then compare activations at that depth. The intervention screen holds the three-layer v3 architecture fixed and changes only covariance correlation, the learning rate schedule, or the reuse of the initial noise draw for each trajectory.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Answer for the Stated Kick}
\label{sec:case}

The quadratic baseline gives $T=0.405\,\mathrm{s}$ and $(Y,Z)=(2.495,1.920)\,\mathrm{m}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}). At $\sigma=5\,\mathrm{mm}$, the calibrated full-mixture MDN (seed 11) gives a mode of $(2.666,1.792)\,\mathrm{m}$ and a mean of $(2.711,1.859)\,\mathrm{m}$. The mode heights for seeds 42 and 155 are approximately $1.83$ and $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The uniform-prior Bayesian reference gives a mode of $(2.650,2.052)\,\mathrm{m}$ and joint region areas of $0.219$ and $0.809\,\mathrm{m^2}$ (ESS $2{,}893$). The two models share flight equations but use different launch distributions and density estimators. Their modes differ in height by about $0.26\,\mathrm{m}$. The earlier uniform-trained P1 model gave a height of $2.15\,\mathrm{m}$ for this input. The comparison changes both the launch distribution and the network, so it does not isolate the effect of either.

\subsection{Paired Comparison with the Physics Posterior}
\label{sec:paired}

We draw 32 trajectories from the v3 test split (seed 155108) and use the same $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ perturbation for each method. Adaptive importance sampling starts with $6{,}000$ proposals per stage and retries with $18{,}000$ when conditional ESS falls below 100. After retry, 30 trajectories meet the ESS threshold and have a finite quadratic prediction. The median Euclidean errors are $0.478\,\mathrm{m}$ for quadratic extrapolation, $0.314\,\mathrm{m}$ for the Bayesian reference and $0.270\,\mathrm{m}$ for the calibrated v3 model (seed 11).

Median energy scores are $0.241$ and $0.173$ for the Bayesian reference and v3 model, respectively. The energy score is $\mathbb E\|\mathbf Q-\mathbf q\|-\tfrac12\mathbb E\|\mathbf Q-\mathbf Q'\|$, with independent predictive draws $\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q'$ and truth $\mathbf q$ \cite{gneiting2007}. Median elapsed times on the same CPU are $2.19\,\mathrm{s}$ and $1.74\,\mathrm{ms}$, including inference, sampling or resampling $4{,}096$ endpoints, and scoring. The small ESS-filtered subset and the different prior distributions limit the accuracy comparison.

\subsection{Rebound and Rolling Crossings}
\label{sec:bounce}

For low shots, we continue the flight when the ball centre reaches $z=R$. The vertical velocity reverses with normal restitution $e_n=0.60$. At impact, the horizontal slip is $\mathbf v_t+(\boldsymbol\omega\times(-R\hat{\mathbf z}))_t$. With thin-shell inertia $I=2mR^2/3$, the tangential impulse opposes slip and is capped at $\mu_c$ times the normal impulse ($\mu_c=0.40$). The impulse changes both horizontal velocity and spin. When the incoming normal speed drops below $0.50\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the ball rolls with resistance $\mu_r g$ ($\mu_r=0.08$) and quadratic drag. We extend the integration horizon to $3\,\mathrm{s}$. These coefficients define the simulated surface.

We use a separate $100{,}000$-launch v3 bank with the full anchor mixture. The simulator preserves the $88{,}999$ direct crossings and recovers $11{,}001$ additional crossings ($6{,}636$ during a rebound and $4{,}365$ while rolling). Of the post-contact crossings, $7{,}761$ are in-frame under the radius-inset criterion. The contact simulator supplies missing positions after impact, completing the 951 observation prefixes left incomplete by the airborne integrator. Varying $e_n$ over $0.56$--$0.67$, $\mu_c$ over $0.25$--$0.55$ and $\mu_r$ over $0.046$--$0.153$ one at a time leaves $11{,}001$ post-contact crossings in each simulated scenario. The in-frame count ranges from $7{,}742$ to $7{,}761$.

Two Wiener residual MDNs have the same architecture as Section~\ref{sec:mdn}, seed 11 and 100 training epochs. The airborne-only model trains on $71{,}202$ direct crossings. The contact-inclusive model trains on the same rows plus $8{,}798$ post-contact crossings. Each model uses its own selection and calibration folds from the same partition. Both are evaluated on the same $10{,}000$ rows with identical noise and receive only the two positions and $\sigma$ through the feature extractor. Ground-contact status defines evaluation subsets. The contact-inclusive model estimates the endpoint density conditional on crossing by either route.

\section{Discussion}

Two noisy positions constrain average velocity more tightly than acceleration. The Wiener anchor shrinks the quadratic estimate more strongly as $\sigma$ increases. The mixture then learns how to correct that anchor under the training distribution. The wind and spin-decay tests show that nominal HDR coverage depends on how closely the flight dynamics match that distribution.

Aiming increases sampling density in the high-speed target grid. The shared uniform-prior test shows a small decrease in median point error and a small increase in NLL. The three source-held-out Asai replays use reduced mixtures and an assumed measurement noise. The DFL and ISSIA checks address individual physical trajectories, including trajectories used to construct the full-mixture prior. Calibration for real penalties remains unmeasured. The earlier P1 results use a uniform test distribution and a ball-centre goal rectangle. Comparison with v3 calls for shared test flights, noise and goal geometry.

The airborne model forecasts geometric in-frame arrival conditional on $E$. The contact-inclusive model conditions on crossing by either route within the simulated horizon. An unconditional in-frame forecast requires a separate estimate of crossing probability. The geometry accounts for ball radius and omits frame contact and goalkeeper intervention. The post-contact comparison depends on the assumed surface parameters and simulated mix of rebounds and rolls.

In a local calculation on held-out v3 launches, an additional $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$ observation with the same $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ noise reduces the median radial crossing spread from $0.284$ to $0.113\,\mathrm{m}$ under a Gaussian reference prior (Appendix~\ref{app:design}). This measurement design adds $50\,\mathrm{ms}$ before the forecast can be made.

\section{Conclusion and Outlook}

At an assumed $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ coordinate noise, the million-trajectory MDN predicts a most likely crossing at $(2.67,1.79)\,\mathrm{m}$ for the contest kick. Its $95\%$ region covers $95.1\%$ of the held-out still-air simulations at this noise level. Crosswind reduces coverage under the fixed model. In the single-seed paired experiment on the specified simulated surface, contact-inclusive training reduces the post-contact median error from $0.242$ to $0.163\,\mathrm{m}$, with a $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ median difference on direct crossings. The post-contact inner region covers $58.9\%$ and $22.0\%$ of its predictive draws fall below the physical height limit. High-rate tracking with independently measured noise and a real holdout spanning direct crossings, misses and ground contact would test these findings.

\subsection{Baseline from the Three Given Positions}

For the positions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:given} and $\Delta t=0.05\,\mathrm{s}$, the midpoint velocity and acceleration are
\begin{align}
  \mathbf v(\Delta t)&=\frac{\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_0}{2\Delta t}=(29.6,8.6,6.3)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\label{eq:baseline}\\
  \mathbf a&=\frac{\mathbf r_2-2\mathbf r_1+\mathbf r_0}{\Delta t^2}=(-16,-16,-12)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.
\end{align}
Writing $\tau=T-\Delta t$, solve $-8\tau^2+29.6\tau-9.5=0$ and take the earlier forward root, $\tau=0.355\,\mathrm{s}$. Substitution gives $T=0.405\,\mathrm{s}$ and
\begin{equation}
  (Y,Z)=(0.45+8.6\tau-8\tau^2,\;0.44+6.3\tau-6\tau^2)=(2.495,1.920)\,\mathrm{m}.
  \label{eq:baselineYZ}
\end{equation}
The benchmark baseline selects the earliest finite quadratic root after $0.10\,\mathrm{s}$, with a linear branch when $a_x$ is near zero.

\subsection{Propagation of Position Noise to the Endpoint}

With an exact start and two independent coordinate errors of variance $\sigma^2$, the second difference has error $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_2-2\boldsymbol\varepsilon_1$ and component variance $5\sigma^2$ \cite{gum2008}. Thus
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_a=\frac{\sqrt{5}\,\sigma}{\Delta t^2}=4.47\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
  \quad\text{at }\sigma=5\,\mathrm{mm}.
  \label{eq:sigmaa}
\end{equation}
An independently noisy start would raise the component variance to $6\sigma^2$.

For a fixed crossing time $T$, let $n=T/\Delta t$. In the quadratic interpolant through the exact start and the two observations, the noisy positions have Lagrange weights $L_1=-n(n-2)$ and $L_2=n(n-1)/2$. The per-axis standard deviation at fixed $T$ is $\Lambda(n)\sigma$, where
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda(n)=\sqrt{L_1^2+L_2^2},\qquad
  e_{50}^{\mathrm{fixed}}=\sqrt{2\ln 2}\,\Lambda(n)\sigma .
  \label{eq:noise-amplification}
\end{equation}
Here $e_{50}^{\mathrm{fixed}}$ is the median radial deviation in $(Y,Z)$ for isotropic Gaussian errors. The stated kick has $n=8.10$ and $\Lambda=57.2$, giving $0.337\,\mathrm{m}$ at $5\,\mathrm{mm}$.

We also propagate the observations through $150{,}000$ quadratic fits per noise level, solving for the crossing time in each draw. This diagnostic requires a positive midpoint-relative root, $T\le1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ and $Z>R$ at the plane. At $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ its median radial deviation from the unperturbed quadratic crossing is $0.339\,\mathrm{m}$.

\subsection{Measurement Design and Local Crossing Spread}
\label{app:design}

We sample $4{,}000$ airborne launches without replacement from the full-mixture v3 test split (seed 155107). Let $\mathbf h(\boldsymbol\theta)$ be the observed positions and $\mathbf q(\boldsymbol\theta)=(Y,Z)$ the crossing. We compute their Jacobians $J_h$ and $J_q$ by centred differences about each simulated launch. With a diagonal Gaussian reference covariance $\Sigma_\theta$ whose variances match the uniform ranges, the local crossing covariance is
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_q=J_q
  \bigl(\sigma^{-2}J_h^\top J_h+\Sigma_\theta^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}
  J_q^\top ,
  \label{eq:local-cov}
\end{equation}
Each prior variance is $(\theta_{\max}-\theta_{\min})^2/12$. We estimate each flight's median radial spread from $64$ draws of $\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\Sigma_q)$ and report the median across flights in Table~\ref{tab:local-spread}. The third-position variant adds a reading at $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$ with independent noise of the same standard deviation. These are local uncertainty estimates under the simulated dynamics and the Gaussian reference prior, which differs from the mixture used for training.
